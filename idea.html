Decision Systems

Why good teams get stuck, and what gets them moving

Most stuck decisions are not short on information. They are short on clear answers to three questions: who decides, what proof is enough, and what happens next. A decision system answers them.

The idea

Information tells you what is true. It does not tell you what to do next.

A model tells you what is likely. A forecast tells you what might happen. Neither tells you who gets to say yes, who carries the loss if it goes wrong, or when the chance to decide runs out. That is the decision system around the work, and it is where expensive projects quietly stall.

I evaluate that system, design a better one, and help build it, so the next step is clear and someone owns it.

How I prove it

I test it on decisions that already played out

I do not ask anyone to take it on faith, and I do not lean on case studies. A case study only shows something worked once. Testing backwards checks the method itself, against decisions where we already know what happened.

01

Take a real decision

One that was already made and already played out: yours, or one from the field.

02

Run it through the system

Who decided, what proof was missing, which way it could have moved, as if deciding it fresh.

03

Compare to what happened

Does the system find a better path than the one the room actually took?

04

Let hindsight judge

When it keeps finding the path that was missed, it works. That is the proof.

In short

If using the system would have led to a better outcome, looking back, it is real.

How it works

Three questions, every time

The full detail is what teams learn in the course, and what the ORDOVANCE Read runs on. The shape is simple.

Ready?

Is it ready to decide?

Seven things must be clear first: the goal, who decides, who benefits, how much risk is acceptable, the deadline, whether the team can carry it out, and when to walk away.

Options

What can it do next?

Not just yes or no. Five honest options: go ahead, wait, change course, take it up a level, or stop.

Pattern

What is going wrong?

Five patterns: on track, drifting, going in circles, pushback, and frozen. Each one needs a different fix. Stopping is not a pattern; it is the right move when there is no responsible way to keep going.

Where it applies

Built for decisions with real stakes, not one industry

The same questions that decide a capital project in enhanced oil recovery decide a sale, a negotiation, or a government contract.

Enhanced oil recovery is where I first proved it in public. It does not end there: wherever money, risk and authority meet a decision that has to move, the same three questions apply.

The fastest way to understand it is to watch it work on a decision you actually care about.
